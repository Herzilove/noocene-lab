# Playful reflection concepts

Current release includes a tappable companion with reviewed hints, text-first input, editable choices, a Reality Lens, tiny missions and an optional locally saved Time Capsule with a later outcome check-in. Garden states show actual saved/revisited entries. These are implemented interactions, not evidence of improved metacognition. Thought Catcher animation, Future Fork and Friend Mode remain proposals.

| Concept | Interaction | Reflection purpose | First useful implementation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Thought Catcher | A written thought becomes a floating bubble; tap it to hold and inspect it. | Notice a thought as an event rather than automatically treating it as fact. | Keep typing accessible; decorate a user-confirmed thought with a tappable bubble. |
| Reality Lens | Flip between what happened and what the mind added. | Separate a reported observation from an interpretation; allow uncertainty. | Two user-editable sides; no automatic true/false judgment about a personal story. |
| Future Fork | Try two possible next actions in a short what-if scene. | Explore options before choosing an action. | Label outcomes hypothetical; never present another person's reaction as known. |
| Time Capsule | Save an expectation and later compare it with what happened. | Make predictions visible and revisit them with fresh evidence. | One optional prediction plus one later outcome; keep both locally and editable. |
| Borrow a Friend's View | Rephrase a moment as if a friend were describing it. | Try psychological distance without assigning a personality label. | Toggle perspectives using the user's own words; research background remains optional. |
| Living Garden | Saved ideas become small plants with captured/tried/revisited states. | Encourage returning to an actual experiment or discovery. | Growth reflects recorded actions, not a fabricated consciousness or wellness score. |

Recommended order: Reality Lens and Time Capsule first, then Thought Catcher. Each adds a clear reflective action, rather than just decoration. Keep a short default path; optional depth should never block completion. Respect keyboard use and reduced-motion preferences.

Possible pilot checks: can people explain observation versus interpretation in their own example; can they complete the flow without help; do they return to record an outcome; do the effects hold beyond novelty? These are candidate evaluation questions, not established benefits.
